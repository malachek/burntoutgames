# Burnt Out Games — Voice & Messaging

Everything the site says comes from this file. It's built from the brand guide's
own reasoning about the flame, Malachy's answers in `Marketing Mentor Notes`, and
how the Steam pages already read.

---

## 1. The thesis

**Fire feeds itself.**

Somebody here gets obsessed with an idea. They carry it far enough that everyone
else catches it. It becomes a game. Someone plays that game, feels how much fun
we were having, and tells us what they want next. That sends us back to the
engine with more than we started with.

That loop is the brand. The flame in the logo has been through the heat and is
still going because it keeps getting fed.

Malachy said the useful version of this out loud in the mentor session: *"we
enjoy playing in development. We want the fun in our dev to show in the fun of
your play."* Everything on the site is downstream of that sentence.

---

## 2. Primary motto

> ## Fuel your fire.

It works in both directions at once, which is why it's the right line. We're
telling players to go chase the thing they're obsessed with. We're also telling
them that playing our games, and telling us about it, is what fuels ours.

Use it as the homepage hero. Use it as a sign-off. Don't dilute it by repeating
it three times on one page.

**The functional descriptor** — meta tags, search results, press, anywhere a
stranger or a machine needs the plain version:

> Burnt Out Games is an independent studio making fast, loud, high-energy games.

**Supporting lines**, for section headers and social. Rotate them.

- We are the players.
- The fun in our dev shows in your play.
- What we lack in resources we make up in freedom.
- No BS, no filler.

All four are Malachy's own lines from the mentor session, lightly tightened. When
a headline is needed, look there first before writing a new one.

---

## 3. The register

Warm, high-energy, generous. Someone who genuinely loves this and wants you in
on it.

**The site is written for a player first.** Gen Z, on a phone, deciding in about
two seconds whether to keep scrolling. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. Say what
you *do* in the game in the first line.

**Publishers read the same page.** They're not looking for a different tone,
they're looking for evidence underneath it: shipped titles with real dates,
correct engine names, working links, a press kit that exists, a team with names
and faces. Competence shows through specificity, so every enthusiastic sentence
is followed by a checkable one. That's how one page serves both.

Where a genuinely calmer register is needed (press kit, boilerplate, fact
sheets), keep the same warmth and drop the volume. Never switch to corporate.

---

## 4. Hard rules

**No "X, not Y."** This is the big one. Constructions like *"it isn't a stat, it's
the thing you're protecting"*, *"not because it's fast, but because it's real"*,
*"less a shooter, more a race"* are the single clearest tell of machine-written
copy, and an earlier draft of this site was full of them. One per page is a
lapse. Two is a rewrite. Say the thing you mean and stop.

Related shapes, all banned: *"X is the whole point"*, *"that's not X. That's Y."*,
*"the through-line is…"*, *"and that's on purpose"*, *"which is exactly why…"*.

**Never sell by attacking other games.** No *"most movement shooters fake it"*,
no *"unlike other studios"*, no genre critique. It reads as insecure, it ages
badly, and it makes a studio with three titles sound like it's picking fights.
Describe what ours does and let it stand.

**Every game bio says what you do.** First sentence: the player's situation and
their verb. Second: what it feels like. Then the specifics. If a paragraph could
sit in a design doc instead of on a store page, it isn't a game bio yet.

**Never volunteer smallness.** No "just a small team", "only students", "we know
it's rough". Being students is a reason we move fast, and it appears that way or
not at all.

**Every claim is checkable.** If a number is on the site, it came from Steam,
itch or GameJolt and you could produce the screenshot.

**No em dashes in body copy.** Use a full stop or a colon.

**One call to action per section.** The funnel is views to Steam to wishlist. A
section with three doors converts on none.

**Banned words:** immersive · passionate · cutting-edge · revolutionary · journey
· experiences (meaning games) · committed to · we strive to · seamlessly ·
game-changing · epic · next-level · unapologetic · visceral.

("Relentless" was on this list and came off it: it is Burnt Out Games' own word
on EXO's Steam page, and the game copy is taken from those pages close to
verbatim. Store copy the studio already wrote outranks this list.)

**Sentence length varies hard.** Player-facing copy runs 4 to 10 words. Press
copy runs 12 to 20. A paragraph of same-length sentences reads as generated.

## 4b. The studio bio

The angle is **what it's like in the room**. Lead with the process, let the games
be the evidence.

> Most of what we make starts as an argument. Someone turns up obsessed with an
> idea, defends it badly, then goes away and builds a prototype that shuts
> everyone up. Three games in, that is still the whole process.

The Studio page runs that out over four paragraphs: the argument, *we are the
players* (we're our own audience, and we're out of time, so nothing ships with
filler), freedom over resources, and the fun in the build showing up in the play.

Two earlier attempts failed and are worth not repeating. The first was abstract
("that energy is the actual product") and said nothing checkable. The second
repeated itself: the heading "Whatever's burning hardest, that's what we build"
and the paragraph under it made the same point twice. A heading sets up the
paragraph or it goes.

## 5. The games

Each one leads with the player's situation. None of them mention another game.

### EXO — third-person speed shooter, in development

- **Tagline:** Speed is your weapon. Souls are your fuel.
- **What you do:** Take enemies down to charge your suit, spend the charge on
  velocity, keep the chain alive across a whole planet.
- **What it feels like:** The horizon bending under you as you go faster.
- **Press version:** "A third-person speed shooter built in Unreal Engine 5, set
  on spherical planets where velocity is the core combat mechanic. In development
  for PC."
- **Call to action:** Wishlist on Steam. One button, nothing competing with it.

### Kawai'ian Isolation — narrative horror, released April 2026, free

- **Tagline:** Welcome to Paradise. Enjoy your stay.
- **What you do:** Explore a resort that keeps rearranging itself around what you
  remember of home.
- **What it feels like:** Somewhere beautiful that is very slightly wrong.
- **Press version:** "A narrative horror game about a pageant winner and the
  resort she can't leave. Released free on Steam, April 2026."

### Burning Out — co-op survival horror, released August 2025, $4.99

- **Tagline:** Four of you. One maze. One torch.
- **What you do:** Find the way out before the fire runs down, staying close
  enough to hear each other on proximity chat.
- **What it feels like:** Four people making bad decisions loudly, in the dark.
- **Press version:** "A four-player co-op horror maze-escape with proximity chat,
  built in Unity. Released on Steam, August 2025."

## 6. Boilerplate

> Burnt Out Games is an independent game studio founded in 2024 by a team of game
> design students. Its released titles are *Burning Out* (2025), a four-player
> co-op horror maze-escape, and *Kawai'ian Isolation* (2026), a free narrative
> horror game. The studio's next title is *EXO*, a third-person speed shooter in
> development for PC. The team works across genres and builds whichever idea has
> the most energy behind it.

**Short version:**

> Burnt Out Games is an independent studio making fast, loud, high-energy games.
> Released: *Burning Out* and *Kawai'ian Isolation*. In development: *EXO*.
